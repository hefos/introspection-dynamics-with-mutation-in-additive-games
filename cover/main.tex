Dear Professor Zaccour,

Editor-in-Chief, \emph{Dynamic Games and Applications}

We are pleased to resubmit our manuscript, ``Introspection Dynamics with Mutation in
Additive Games,'' by Harry Foster, Vince Knight, and Sebastian Krapohl, for
consideration in \emph{Dynamic Games and Applications}.

This is a revised version of a manuscript we submitted earlier (Submission ID
34ef12bd-848d-4588-98bd-3a6eee8486af) and asked to pause. After submission, Marta
Couto and Saptarshi Pal kindly pointed out that the product-measure decomposition for
additive games, which the earlier version had presented as a new result, was already
established in their 2023 paper in this journal (their Proposition~2). We are grateful
for this correction. We have revised the manuscript accordingly: we attribute the
decomposition to Couto and Pal throughout, and we reposition our contribution as the
extension of introspection dynamics to \emph{mutation} and player-specific selection
intensities, together with the structural consequences that follow. We set out this
explanation here at your request.

Introspection dynamics, a learning dynamic for asymmetric multiplayer games, was
introduced by Couto et al.\ (2022) and extended to multiplayer settings by Couto and
Pal (2023), the latter published in this journal. Couto and Pal showed that for
\emph{additive} games, those in which the payoff difference a player evaluates when
switching action is independent of the co-players' actions, the stationary distribution
of introspection dynamics is a product measure, with the linear public goods game as
their worked example. We extend this analysis to introspection dynamics with
\emph{mutation} and with player-specific selection intensities. We give a short, direct
proof that the product-measure structure survives, derive a closed-form cooperation
probability for the heterogeneous public goods game, and show that mutation reshapes
long-run cooperation in a simple way: aggregate cooperation is affine in the mutation
rate, interpolating between the selection-driven level and neutral drift. The closed form
also remains valid in the strong-selection limit, where the mutation-free dynamics
degenerate.

The paper is structured as a concise, self-contained exposition: we describe the dynamic,
establish the additivity condition under which the exact formula holds, and provide
proofs. We believe the contribution is a natural complement to the foundational work on
introspection dynamics, in particular Couto and Pal (2023), and is well suited to
\emph{Dynamic Games and Applications}.
